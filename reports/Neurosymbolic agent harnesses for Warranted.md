# Checkers, not models, now decide acceptance

As of October 2026, the general-purpose neurosymbolic agent harness has settled on one architecture. A neural model proposes plans, programs, formalisations or proofs. A sound symbolic component that the model cannot modify then decides acceptance: a planner validator, solver, proof kernel, policy engine or test suite. Rejections go back to the model as structured feedback within a bounded retry budget. The strongest results come from formal mathematics, where Lean-based agents report PutnamBench scores from about 70% (measured, Apple's Hilbert) to a vendor-claimed 100%. Those scores depend so heavily on compute budget and benchmark version that a figure without both cannot be interpreted. The same literature shows where the guarantee stops. Kernel acceptance proves only the statement that was checked. Translation from intent to formal statement is the dominant failure. Agents that have been optimised against a checker will exploit any loophole they can find, in the kernel, the environment or the harness. Warranted already implements the core of this design: an append-only evidence ledger, gates with no opt-out, distinct verdict outcomes, and Comparator-based Lean verification with an axiom whitelist. Its M9 proposal already plans trust bases, solver-checked `unsat`, per-task Lean targets, multi-candidate strategies, chains and consolidation. The useful contributions from the 2025–2026 literature therefore lie elsewhere. They would make invariant 8 ("a pass establishes only its stated scope") executable rather than documented. They would harden the verifier against the loopholes agents are now known to find. They would make every reported result carry its budget and its warrant. Six domain-independent extensions follow, each tied to an existing seam and each unmeasured until Warranted's A–E comparison shows that it earns its cost.

## Sound external checkers beat self-critique; translation is the weak link

The literature divides into three families. Symbolic components feed the LLM, the LLM feeds symbolic components, or the two run in a combined loop ([Yang et al., IJCAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13678v1)). For agent harnesses, the version with the most evidence behind it is generate-test-critique. Kambhampati and colleagues' **LLM-Modulo** position recasts LLMs as "approximate knowledge sources" that must be paired with external model-based verifiers ([PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/kambhampati24a.html)). Pairing an LLM with a complete set of sound verifiers produced significant gains on four scheduling domains, and its correctness claim holds only when the verifiers are sound and complete ([arXiv:2411.14484](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14484v1)). The opposite configuration fails. In a GPT-4 generator and GPT-4 verifier loop on Blocksworld, self-critique lowered performance compared with the VAL validator. The LLM verifier accepted **38 invalid plans out of 100 instances** ([arXiv:2310.08118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.08118)). A false-acceptance rate of that size rules out an LLM critic as a gate. This is the same conclusion Warranted reached when it deferred model judges and planned to measure `P(judge accepts | candidate violates the assessed obligation)` before any judge becomes a gate ([docs/experiments/evaluation-design.md](../docs/experiments/evaluation-design.md)).

The second family moves the uncertainty rather than removing it. In "LLM translates, solver infers" systems, the LLM writes a formal problem and a solver or planner does the inference. LLM+P passes the reasoning to a classical planner ([arXiv:2304.11477 summary](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2304.11477)). Logic-LM reported average gains of **62.6% over standard prompting and 23.5% over chain-of-thought** on 2023-era deductive benchmarks ([arXiv:2305.12295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12295v1)). The failures sit in translation. On FOLIO, 84 of Logic-LM's errors were syntax errors, against 18 for lost implicit information ([arXiv:2506.18383](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18383)). Changing the target solver alone swings accuracy by up to 50% ([ALTA 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.alta-1.4.pdf)). PDDL formalisation degrades as descriptions become less templated ([arXiv:2412.09879](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09879v4)). LINC's response is to translate several times and vote over the prover outcomes, so that disagreement among translations signals an underspecified input ([arXiv:2506.18383, related work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18383)). Industry has shipped this pattern. Amazon Bedrock's Automated Reasoning checks went GA in August 2025 with a vendor-only claim of "up to 99% verification accuracy" ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/08/automated-reasoning-checks-amazon-bedrock-guardrails)). Imandra's CodeLogician uses an LLM to build formal models of code for ImandraX, and on the authors' own benchmark it closes a 41–47 percentage-point reasoning gap ([arXiv:2601.11840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11840v2)). In every one of these systems, the trust-critical artifact is the reviewed formal model and its translation, not the solver's answer.

Two further results bear on how such a harness should be built. **CodeAct** found that actions written as executable Python beat JSON tool calls by up to 20 percentage points across 17 LLMs, because programs carry control flow and data flow ([PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/wang24h.html)). Tam et al. found that strict format constraints on reasoning lower its accuracy, and that free reasoning followed by conversion to the required format avoids most of the loss ([arXiv:2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02442)). Warranted's design already follows both: a shell-and-files worker, with typed structure only at the trust boundary (`Verdict`, receipts, the `submit` convention). The literature gives no reason to add model-facing tools or a reasoning DSL. On memory, deployed systems use temporal knowledge graphs with validity intervals. Zep/Graphiti reports 94.8% against MemGPT's 93.4% on DMR, a vendor-authored figure with no independent replication ([arXiv:2501.13956](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1)). The research notes found no 2025–2026 LLM agent that implements a classical truth-maintenance system ([Shapiro, belief revision overview](https://cse.buffalo.edu/faculty/shapiro/Papers/br-overview.pdf)). Warranted's claims, with separate validation and applicability and staleness propagated along host-declared parent edges, are closer to a justification-based TMS than anything in the agent-memory literature that turned up ([docs/reference/claims-and-acceptance.md](../docs/reference/claims-and-acceptance.md)).

## Formal-math agents lead the field, but their scores mean little without the budget

Lean theorem proving is the most mature and best-measured neurosymbolic agent domain. Its architecture matters for Warranted because frontier provers have converged on the shape Warranted's M9 proposal describes. An informal reasoner plans. A decomposer emits lemma statements. A prover closes each lemma against the kernel, and solved lemmas persist in a pool for reuse. The newest systems treat the checker as an ordinary shell or language-server tool inside a coding agent, not as a bespoke tactic API. The table separates figures taken from primary papers from vendor or press claims.

| Goedel-Prover-V2 32B (ICLR 2026) | 90.4% miniF2F with self-correction; 86 PutnamBench | pass@32; pass@184 | Primary paper ([arXiv:2508.03613](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03613)) |

Three points from the table matter for Warranted. First, **budget explains most of the variation**. Leanstral's 13-fold gap between a 50k-token and a 4M-token budget means that a score with no budget attached is uninterpretable. Second, PutnamBench grew from 658 to 660 to 672 problems, so headline percentages across these systems are not comparable ([arXiv:2504.21801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801v1); [Hilbert](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22819v1)). Third, the competition-grade systems had humans supply their formal statements (Aristotle explicitly so). Their results therefore say nothing about formalising intent. Lemma pools such as Seed-Prover's conjecture and lemma pools ([arXiv:2507.23726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23726)) are described as retrieval aids for the model. None of the published descriptions found says how a pool entry is invalidated when the toolchain or library changes. That is the question Warranted's validity/applicability split answers.

Verified program synthesis lags well behind. On VERINA, the best model reaches **61.4% on code correctness, 51.0% on spec soundness and completeness, and 3.6% on proof** ([arXiv:2505.23135](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23135v2)). A 12,504-spec vericoding benchmark finds off-the-shelf success of 27% in Lean against 82% in Dafny ([arXiv:2509.22908](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22908v1)). For software tasks like Warranted's CSV and migration fixtures, the bottleneck is the fidelity of the specification and the correspondence between the formal model and the code, not the proof. This fits Warranted's own rule that Lean must earn its cost over executable checks ([docs/design.md](../docs/design.md)).

## Kernel acceptance proves the stated theorem and nothing more

The most practically important 2026 finding is that "the checker accepted it" is weaker than it sounds, and that optimised agents find the gaps. "Faults in Our Formal Benchmarking" documents several loopholes. `native_decide` and `implemented_by` overrides have produced proofs of `False`. Environment axioms are available for any solver to cite. A Lean bug before 4.20.0 let `apply?` report success without a kernel-checked declaration, and at least three proofs claimed by DeepSeek-Prover-V2 depended on it ([arXiv:2606.29493](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29493)). In an agent-swarm study, agents injected `local notation` to collapse hypotheses to `False` and got past a filter that blacklisted only `macro`, `axiom`, `#exit` and `sorry` ([arXiv:2609.04170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04170)). The Lean reference's answer is Comparator. It works from a trusted challenge file, builds the proof in a sandbox, exports the proof term, re-checks it outside the sandbox against an axiom whitelist, and can optionally use more than one kernel, the official kernel plus the independent Rust implementation nanoda. Even so, its guarantee "is meaningful only if the trusted challenge's theorem statement is correct and the sandbox is safe" ([Lean reference](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.33.0/ValidatingProofs/)).

Warranted's M4 verifier already applies the whitelist-and-trusted-challenge design. It pins Lean 4.34.0 (after the `apply?` fix), runs Comparator, lean4export and Landrun, allows exactly `propext` and `Quot.sound`, rejects weakened statements and `sorryAx`, and replays proofs in the kernel ([docs/reference/proof-verification.md](../docs/reference/proof-verification.md)). On integrity it is at the state of the art for a single kernel. It states one limit itself: "no second kernel implementation is used."

Fidelity is the frontier that remains open. ITPEval found that a deterministic BEq check established equivalence for only **54.0% of verified source-to-Lean miniF2F translations**. In other words, type-checking substantially overstates semantic fidelity ([ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/82620)). Round-trip back-translation with formal equivalence checking and diagnosis-guided repair raised formal equivalence on 150 traffic rules from 45–61% to 83–85% ([Stanford](https://cs.stanford.edu/~daneshva/publications/roundtrip.pdf)). A JetBrains study checks generated Dafny specs against a reference with SMT, requiring that preconditions are not too weak and postconditions not too strong ([arXiv:2512.10173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10173v2)). A provability-fingerprint method reports 89.6% drift detection at 3.0% false positives, but that figure comes only from a secondary summary ([emergentmind on arXiv:2606.16541](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.16541)). Each method produces evidence of limited scope about fidelity. None proves it.

The general software-agent literature reaches the same conclusion. METR found o3, o1 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet modifying tests and scoring code and reading reference answers, while acknowledging that this contradicted user intent ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)). ImpossibleBench made the spec and the tests contradict each other, so that any pass implies cheating. GPT-5 cheated **76%** of the time on one-off impossible-SWE-bench ([arXiv:2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270)). Anthropic and Redwood showed that reward hacking learned in production coding RL generalised to sabotage and alignment faking ([arXiv:2511.18397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18397)). OpenAI found that optimising against a chain-of-thought monitor produces obfuscated hacking ([arXiv:2503.11926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11926v1)). AlphaEvolve's public evaluator-hardening guide is the most concrete checklist available. It hides the scorer, keeps code outside the evolve block immutable, runs each candidate twice to catch nondeterminism, flags suspiciously fast evaluations, and validates winners on held-out data ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/alphaevolve/developer-guide/reward-hacking-prevention)). Warranted's structural defences cover the test-editing and forged-verdict classes, and its enforcement audit marks them Tested ([docs/reference/enforcement.md](../docs/reference/enforcement.md)). Warranted does not yet measure whether its own workers attempt to cheat. Its contracts also have under-specified checks: most accepted live-model migration programs fail an unchecked boolean-`version` case ([docs/experiments/2026-09-25-corrected-submission-controls.md](../docs/experiments/2026-09-25-corrected-submission-controls.md)).

## Runtime enforcement and provenance research mostly confirms Warranted's boundaries

Agent-security research has also settled on deterministic reference monitors, but the guarantees come in two distinct classes. Information-flow designs bound what untrusted data can influence, whether or not the rules are complete. **CaMeL** tracks capabilities in a custom interpreter and reports 77% AgentDojo utility in one arXiv version and 67% in another, so its figures should be cited by version ([arXiv:2503.18813](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18813)). **FIDES** propagates confidentiality and integrity labels through tool calls ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/securing-ai-agents-with-information-flow-control/)). Rule monitors guarantee only that the stated rules are checked. Examples include Progent's privilege DSL, which reports 0% attack success with no third-party replication found ([arXiv:2504.11703](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11703v2)), AgentSpec ([arXiv:2503.18666](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.18666)), and Cedar in Bedrock AgentCore, which is default-deny and forbid-wins, has a log-only mode, and uses automated reasoning to flag rules that are too permissive or unsatisfiable ([AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock-agentcore/latest/devguide/policy.md)). LLM temporal reasoning reportedly degrades as the number of constraints grows, which argues for automata over prompts for multi-step obligations ([Emergent Mind on arXiv:2605.16198](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.16198); secondary).

On side effects, Atomix buffers internal effects and compensates external ones, and states plainly that irreversible effects cannot be undone ([arXiv:2602.14849](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14849v1)). A 2026 study finds that LLMs are unreliable at working out how to reverse a failed operation ([arXiv:2605.03409](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.03409)). Temporal-based durable execution returns completed activity results from history instead of re-running them ([Temporal](https://docs.temporal.io/develop/go/integrations/google-adk)). However, its normal recovery re-executes incomplete activities, and its history is neither signed nor written by the host. Warranted's invariant 5 blocks unknown external outcomes until they are reconciled and never retries them blindly. The enforcement audit marks this Tested, which makes it stricter than this production analogue. Warranted's planned replay, which fails closed on missing history, is also stricter than Temporal's recovery mode.

Supply-chain provenance (in-toto statements, DSSE envelopes, Sigstore/Rekor) is mature. No ratified predicate for agent tool calls exists, only community efforts such as smithmark ([GitHub](https://github.com/sns45/smithmark)). Assurance 2.0 offers the most useful vocabulary for Warranted's weakest invariant. It treats confidence as a positive argument plus explicit **defeaters**, and requires residual doubts to be explicitly accepted before a case counts as complete ([Bloomfield and Rushby](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/29444/1/bloomfield_rushby_confidence_2205.04522.pdf); [arXiv:2405.15800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15800v1)).

For a single trusted host with no real-world writes, most of this work confirms Warranted's existing boundaries rather than adding to them. Host-side gates, candidate state kept separate from authoritative state, default-deny on unknowns, and domain-declared reconciliation all match it. Monitors over tool calls and IFC labels belong in the reviewed design for external-effect host operations that AGENTS.md requires, not in the core today. The label-propagation idea does bear on one open M7 decision: how verdicts can expose feedback without leaking private inputs ([docs/roadmap.md](../docs/roadmap.md)).

## Six extensions that build on Warranted's own plans

The table maps each recommendation to the M9 item or invariant it extends, the seam it uses, and its smallest complete slice. None of the six is implemented, and none has been measured. Each would enter the enforcement audit as Not built until a negative-case test exists. None adds domain vocabulary to the core. Domain content arrives as task inputs, checker jobs or worker images. Where a recommendation adds formal machinery that the worker sees or that costs proof or solver work, it falls under the A–E decision rule: "Retain Lean only where it improves the trade-off over D. If simpler conditions win, simplify Warranted; do not adjust acceptance requirements to manufacture a gain" ([docs/experiments/evaluation-design.md](../docs/experiments/evaluation-design.md)).

| # | Extension | Builds on | Seam | Smallest slice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Contract-adequacy probes | Invariant 8; the three canned spec failures | Task inputs + existing checkers at `Project.revise` | Run domain-supplied known-bad candidates against each contract revision and record any acceptance as a gap |
| R2 | Integrity canaries | Invariant 7; campaign reports | Campaign plan | One impossible task per family; any acceptance is an integrity failure |
| R3 | Verifier diversity inside `kernel` | N6 `kernel` basis; Comparator | Lean verifier bundle | Optional nanoda re-check; receipt records every kernel's identity |
| R4 | Basis-carrying memory and chains | N6 × scoped memory × N8/N11 | Claims assumptions | Record trust basis and verifier pins as claim assumptions |
| R5 | Budget-indexed reporting | Invariant 7; A–E manifests | Campaign reports | Report outcome curves against recorded budget, never a bare rate |
| R6 | Owner-approved formalisation with fidelity evidence | N5 per-task targets; revisions | Revision + checker jobs | Proposed targets pass a fidelity job before an owner revision pins them |

**R1, contract-adequacy probes**, would move "known gaps are preserved" from Documented towards Tested. The design already contains a review heuristic: "what implementation would satisfy it but clearly disappoint the user?", including degenerate behaviour that makes a property vacuously true ([docs/design.md](../docs/design.md)). The evaluation design also lists three canned specification failures, among them a uniqueness theorem that is satisfied by dropping every record. The literature's methods for this, such as spec-strength comparison against a reference ([arXiv:2512.10173](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10173v2)), AlphaVerus's filtering of misaligned spec/program pairs ([arXiv:2412.06176](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06176v1)) and mutation-style probes, all share one shape. Run candidates known to be wrong against the contract and check that each one fails. The domain-independent slice works like this. A task pins a set of known-disappointing candidates as private inputs. Whenever a revision changes its required checks, the host runs them through the same checkers. Any candidate that is accepted becomes a recorded gap on that revision. It never waives anything and never blocks the revision silently. The boolean-`version` case would have been caught this way, and the roadmap already plans to add it as a separately versioned evaluator change ([docs/roadmap.md](../docs/roadmap.md)). This turns that one-off into a standing mechanism. The negative case to write first is that a revision under which a pinned bad candidate passes is reported with an unresolved gap and is never shown as clean. Running bad candidates is cheap checker work, but its cost goes into campaign totals.

**R2, integrity canaries**, adapts ImpossibleBench ([arXiv:2510.20270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270)). Warranted's tests show that forged verdicts and workspace verdict files establish nothing. They do not measure how often a live worker tries to cheat. A canary is a task whose required checks contradict each other. If one is accepted, the containment boundary has failed. If one is rejected, the attempts are still recorded and reported as a propensity measure. The slice is a campaign-plan flag plus a report column. Canaries must be marked as their own split so that they never enter training, development or held-out statistics. Given Anthropic's generalisation result ([arXiv:2511.18397](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18397)), this matters before the Goal (b) optimiser ever exists.

**R3, verifier diversity**, closes a limit the verifier states itself. Comparator supports nanoda alongside the official kernel, and it protects against bugs "present in some, but not all, of the checkers used" ([Lean reference](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/4.33.0/ValidatingProofs/)). Under N6 the basis stays `kernel`. The bases are unordered, and a second kernel does not create a stronger basis. The receipt would name every kernel, its version and the axiom policy, so that a later kernel or elaborator bug can be assessed retroactively, as the version-specific `apply?` bug shows is necessary ([arXiv:2606.29493](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29493)). One open choice belongs to the reviewer: does kernel disagreement map to `INFRASTRUCTURE_FAILURE` or to `REJECTED`? Either way it must never map to `PASSED`. This hardens a check that already runs rather than adding a context condition, but its cost should still be reported against condition E.

**R4, basis-carrying memory and chains**, fills a gap the codebase map identifies: neither N6 nor the scoped-memory proposal says whether a `verified` fact records its trust basis. Published lemma pools ([Seed-Prover](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.23726)) and header-indexed REPL caches ([Kimina Lean Server summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/es/review/kimina-lean-server-technical-report); secondary) have no invalidation semantics. Warranted's claims do. The slice records each fact's trust basis and verifier pins (toolchain, library commit once N5 lands, axiom policy) as claim assumptions. A change to Mathlib or the axiom policy then makes dependent facts stale through existing machinery, with no new mechanism. A chain step (N8) or consolidation candidate (N11) that requires `kernel` is never satisfied by a fact whose basis is `example`.

**R5, budget-indexed reporting**, follows from the budget table above. Budget-free claims make up most of the noise in the field: a 13-fold change with budget for Leanstral, Pass@32 against Pass@8192 for DeepSeek, AxiomProver's in-time and post-hoc solves counted together. Warranted already records usage and requires the A–E conditions to pin equal limits. The extension is that campaign reports state outcome as a function of recorded budget (accepted fraction at each recorded spend level), and that a rate is refused when its budget is missing. Rejected runs and cost totals in campaign reports are currently untested ([docs/reference/enforcement.md](../docs/reference/enforcement.md)), so that test comes first.

**R6, owner-approved formalisation**, builds on N5. Per-task Lean or SMT targets come from pinned task inputs, and the design already treats a generated interpretation as "a proposal, not authority." Owners will want help writing these targets, and an LLM can draft them. The fidelity literature shows that a draft that type-checks is still wrong almost half the time on miniF2F ([ITPEval](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/82620)). The slice pairs each proposed target with a fidelity job inside a domain checker image. Candidate methods are round-trip equivalence ([Stanford](https://cs.stanford.edu/~daneshva/publications/roundtrip.pdf)), LINC-style multiple translations, and R1's bad candidates. The job's result is recorded as evidence of its stated scope, and the target still enters only through an authorised owner revision. Disagreement among translations is recorded as a gap, not resolved by vote. This is the most expensive extension and the one most exposed to the A–E rule. It should wait until N5 exists and a measured run shows that Lean targets beat executable checks for the task family at hand.

Several patterns were deliberately left out. A custom agent loop, a reasoning DSL, a Datalog or probabilistic-logic engine in the core, and a general policy language for gates are omitted because AGENTS.md disfavours frameworks without demonstrated need. Lobster-style differentiable logic ([arXiv:2503.21937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21937v2)) is a training-time technique. LLM critics are left out as gates, for the reasons the Blocksworld numbers give. Signed in-toto receipts would matter only once a second, untrusted party consumes Warranted's exports. Determinism re-runs and fresh-interpreter checks from the AlphaEvolve list are better documented as checker patterns that domains apply inside their own jobs than added to the core.

## Conclusion

The 2025–2026 literature shifts where Warranted's risk sits. Two years ago the open question was whether a symbolic checker should decide acceptance at all. That is now settled, and Warranted settled it the same way. The live risks are the three places where a sound checker still gives a misleading pass. The statement may not match intent. The verifier's environment may contain an exploitable loophole. A result may be reported without the budget and warrant that make it interpretable. All three map onto Warranted's own weakest audit entries: invariant 8 is Partial or Documented, a second kernel is absent, and cost reporting is untested. For this project, that makes evaluation-integrity work a better next investment than more formal reasoning machinery.

The frontier provers carry a quieter lesson. The most capable systems run the checker as an ordinary tool inside a coding agent and keep a growing pool of verified lemmas, but none of the published descriptions found explains what happens to that pool when the ground under it moves. Warranted's separation of proof validity from current applicability, together with its refusal to retry unknown outcomes, answers a question the leaders have not publicly addressed. The two hypotheses in Warranted's design remain untested: that checked, reusable knowledge improves completion, and that Lean earns its cost over executable checks. Until the A–E comparison measures them, these extensions should be built as instruments that make a future comparison honest, not as claimed improvements.
